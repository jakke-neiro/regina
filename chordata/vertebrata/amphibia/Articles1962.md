## Context
 
- **The big 1962 event** was the publication of *Regeneration* (ed. Dorothea Rudnick; Ronald Press, New York), the proceedings of the 20th Growth Symposium of the Society for the Study of Development and Growth, held in Williamstown, Mass., June 1961. It contains several classic amphibian chapters (Hay, Rose, Reyer).
- **Dominant questions** were where blastema cells come from (dedifferentiation vs. reserve cells), whether limb tissues keep their identity during regeneration, and how nerves and the apical epidermal cap control blastema formation.
- **Methods** were histology, early electron microscopy, tritiated-thymidine autoradiography, X-irradiation, denervation and grafting.
- **Old species names:** *Triturus viridescens* = *Notophthalmus viridescens* (red-spotted newt); *Triturus pyrrhogaster* = *Cynops pyrrhogaster*; *Amblystoma* = *Ambystoma*; *Siredon mexicanum* = axolotl. *A. punctatum* = *A. maculatum*. 
## Annotated list
 
### Limb: cell origins and differentiation
 
1. **Fischman DA, Hay ED.** Origin of osteoclasts from mononuclear leucocytes in regenerating newt limbs. *Anat Rec* 143(4):329–337 (Aug 1962). [doi:10.1002/ar.1091430402](https://doi.org/10.1002/ar.1091430402) · [PMID 13893183](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/13893183/) [*Triturus (Notophthalmus) viridescens*]
   Autoradiography shows that osteoclasts in the regenerating limb form by fusion of blood-derived mononuclear leucocytes, not by dedifferentiation of stump tissue. Still cited in modern work on osteoclasts and macrophages in salamander regeneration (e.g., Riquelme-Guzmán et al. 2022; Godwin et al. 2013).
2. **Hay ED.** Cytological studies of dedifferentiation and differentiation in regenerating amphibian limbs. In: Rudnick D (ed.), *Regeneration*, pp. 177–210. Ronald Press, New York (1962). [*Ambystoma* larvae; *Triturus*]
   Classic synthesis of Hay's electron-microscope and autoradiographic work arguing that blastema cells arise by dedifferentiation of stump tissues. Book chapter; no DOI.
3. **Pietsch P.** Independence of chondrogenesis from myogenesis during limb regeneration in *Amblystoma* larvae. *J Exp Zool* 150(2):119–127 (Jul 1962). [doi:10.1002/jez.1401500205](https://doi.org/10.1002/jez.1401500205)
   Experimental study showing that cartilage differentiation in the regenerate does not depend on muscle formation.
4. **Rose SM.** Tissue-arc control of regeneration in the amphibian limb. In: Rudnick D (ed.), *Regeneration*. Ronald Press, New York (1962).
   Rose's theory that regeneration is controlled by interactions between tissue types arranged in arcs around the limb. Page numbers not confirmed.
### Limb: nerves and the wound epidermis
 
5. **Thornton CS, Steen TP.** Eccentric blastema formation in aneurogenic limbs of *Ambystoma* larvae following epidermal cap deviation. *Dev Biol* 5(2):328–343 (Oct 1962). [doi:10.1016/0012-1606(62)90017-9](https://doi.org/10.1016/0012-1606(62)90017-9)
   Displacing the apical epidermal cap in nerve-free (aneurogenic) limbs causes the blastema to form off-center under the cap, showing the cap's role in positioning the blastema independently of nerves.
   > Related: Steen's undergraduate honors thesis on the same experiments (Kenyon College, 1962), [digital.kenyon.edu/honorstheses/327](https://digital.kenyon.edu/honorstheses/327).
6. **Liversage RA.** Regeneration of homoplastically deplanted forelimbs following spinal cord ablation in *Amblystoma opacum* larvae. *J Exp Zool* 151(1):1–15 (Oct 1962). [doi:10.1002/jez.1401510102](https://doi.org/10.1002/jez.1401510102)
   Tests the nerve requirement for regeneration using forelimbs transplanted after removal of the spinal cord.
### Eye / lens
 
7. **Reyer RW.** Regeneration in the amphibian eye. In: Rudnick D (ed.), *Regeneration*, pp. 211–265. Ronald Press, New York (1962). [newts, salamanders]
   Major review of Wolffian lens regeneration from the dorsal iris and of retina regeneration. Book chapter; no DOI.
8. **(Author to verify).** Studies on the lens-regeneration in the larval newt lacking pigment of the eye. *Embryologia* / *Dev Growth Differ* (1962). [newt, probably *Cynops (Triturus) pyrrhogaster*]
   Lens regeneration in larvae without eye pigment. Found only as a reference in a 1963 *Dev Biol* paper.
   > ⚠️ Author, volume and pages not confirmed.
### The 1962 Rudnick symposium volume
 
9. **Rudnick D (ed.).** *Regeneration* (20th Symposium of the Society for the Study of Development and Growth). Ronald Press, New York (1962). v + 272 pp.
   Salamander-relevant chapters are listed above as entries 2, 4 and 7. The volume also has plant (Steeves) and invertebrate chapters (e.g., Rasmont on sponges), which are outside scope.
### Other
 
10. **Skowron S.** Research report of the Department of Experimental Zoology and of the Department of Biology and Embryology, 1959–1961. *Folia Biol (Krakow)* 8:396–405 (1962).
    Departmental report from Kraków, cited in modern comparative reviews of salamander limb regeneration (Dwaraka & Voss 2021).
    > ⚠️ Borderline: a report, not a research article; regeneration content not checked.
11. **Steen TP.** Eccentric blastema formation in aneurogenic limbs of *Ambystoma* larvae following epidermal cap deviation. Honors thesis, Kenyon College (1962). [Link](https://digital.kenyon.edu/honorstheses/327)
    The undergraduate thesis behind entry 5. Include only if you count theses.

## Excluded or near-miss items
 
- **Hay ED, Fischman DA.** Origin of the blastema in regenerating limbs of the newt *Triturus viridescens*. *Dev Biol* 3:26–59 — **1961**, not 1962 (often cited alongside entry 1).
- **Dent JN (1962).** Limb regeneration in larvae and metamorphosing individuals of the South African clawed toad — **frog (*Xenopus*)**, not salamander.
- **Wolff E.** Chapter in Rudnick (ed.) *Regeneration*, pp. 53–84 (1962) — appears to be on **planarians**; check before including.
- **Yamada T et al.** Nuclear RNA synthesis in newt iris cells engaged in regenerative transformation into lens cells. *Dev Biol* 7:595–604 — **1963**.
## Caveats
 
- **Coverage is thin.** These are the items I could confirm from PubMed, publisher records and citing papers. A 1962 PubMed search plus a hand-check of the journal tables of contents above will add more, especially from *J Exp Zool*, *J Morphol*, *Growth* and non-English journals (French, German, Russian, Japanese, Polish).
- **No abstracts.** Most entries have no online abstract, so annotations are based on titles, how later papers cite them, and well-known content (e.g., Hay's chapter). Read the originals before relying on the summaries.
- **Still to verify:** entry 8 (author and citation details), page numbers for entry 4, and the regeneration content of entry 10.
