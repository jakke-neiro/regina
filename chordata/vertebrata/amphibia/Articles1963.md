# Salamander Regeneration Literature, 1963
 
An annotated bibliography of salamander regeneration publications from 1963, with a reproducible search strategy.
 
> **Scope note:** This is a small, verified starter set (9 items), not a complete list. 1963 papers are poorly indexed online: most have no abstract in PubMed, and much European, Soviet and Japanese output is not digitized. Use the queries below and the reference lists of these papers to extend it.

## Context
 
- **Limb work in 1963** focused on what controls blastema formation: the amount of nerve supply (Singer's lab), the role of skin and its layers (Glade), and pituitary hormones (Wilkerson).
- **A biochemical turn.** Histochemical studies of enzymes in the regenerating limb (Schmidt; Cohen; Johnson & Singer) were common, an early attempt to describe regeneration in metabolic terms.
- **Lens regeneration** moved toward molecular-era questions. Yamada's group used autoradiography to show that iris cells switch on RNA and then protein synthesis before turning into lens cells, and Eguchi began electron-microscope studies of iris depigmentation.
- **Old species names:** *Triturus viridescens* = *Diemictylus viridescens* = *Notophthalmus viridescens* (red-spotted newt); *Triturus pyrrhogaster* = *Cynops pyrrhogaster*; *Siredon mexicanum* = axolotl; *Amblystoma* = *Ambystoma*.
## Search strategy
 
### PubMed
 
```text
(salamander*[tiab] OR axolotl*[tiab] OR Ambystoma[tiab] OR Amblystoma[tiab] OR Siredon[tiab] OR newt*[tiab] OR Triturus[tiab] OR Diemictylus[tiab] OR Pleurodeles[tiab] OR Taricha[tiab] OR Urodela[tiab] OR Urodela[mh] OR Salamandridae[mh] OR Ambystoma[mh])
AND (regenerat*[tiab] OR Regeneration[mh])
AND 1963[dp]
```
 
Pre-1966 records come from OLDMEDLINE / Index Medicus and mostly have **no abstract**. Note the added term `Diemictylus`, a name for the red-spotted newt used in this period.
 
### Other sources
 
- **Web of Science Core Collection:** `TS=(salamander* OR axolotl* OR Ambystoma OR Amblystoma OR newt* OR Triturus OR Diemictylus OR Urodela) AND TS=(regenerat*)`, `PY=1963`. Title-only for this era.
- **Journal tables of contents for 1963:** *J Exp Zool* (vols. 152–154), *Dev Biol* (vols. 6–8), *J Morphol*, *Anat Rec*, *J Embryol Exp Morphol*, *Growth*, *Embryologia*, *Am Zool* (meeting abstracts), *Roux' Archiv*.
## Annotated list
 
### Limb: nerves, skin and hormones
 
1. **Glade RW.** Effects of tail skin, epidermis, and dermis on limb regeneration in *Triturus viridescens* and *Siredon mexicanum*. *J Exp Zool* 152(2):169–193 (Mar 1963). [doi:10.1002/jez.1401520207](https://doi.org/10.1002/jez.1401520207) [newt; axolotl]
   Grafts tail skin, or its epidermis or dermis alone, onto limbs before amputation to test how skin of different origin affects regeneration. One of the few 1963 papers to compare a newt with the axolotl directly.
2. **Singer M, Mutterperl E.** Nerve requirements for regeneration in forelimb transplants of the newt *Triturus*. *Dev Biol* 7:180–191 (1963). [doi:10.1016/0012-1606(63)90116-7](https://doi.org/10.1016/0012-1606(63)90116-7) [*Triturus (Notophthalmus) viridescens*]
   Uses transplanted forelimbs to test how much nerve supply is needed for regeneration, part of Singer's work on a quantitative nerve threshold.
3. **Wilkerson JA.** The role of growth hormone in regeneration of the forelimb of the hypophysectomized newt. *J Exp Zool* 154(2):223–230 (Nov 1963). [doi:10.1002/jez.1401540209](https://doi.org/10.1002/jez.1401540209) [*Triturus viridescens*]
   Tests whether growth hormone can restore limb regeneration in newts whose pituitary has been removed.
### Limb: histochemistry and metabolism
 
4. **Schmidt AJ.** Succinic, malic, α-glycerophosphate and lactic dehydrogenases in limb tissues of the adult newt, *Diemictylus viridescens*. *J Exp Zool* 153:69–80 (1963). [doi:10.1002/jez.1401530108](https://doi.org/10.1002/jez.1401530108)
   Maps dehydrogenase enzymes in newt limb tissues, as the baseline for Schmidt's follow-up studies of the regenerating limb (e.g., *J Exp Zool* 1964).
   > ⚠️ Borderline: the title covers normal limb tissues; check how much is on regeneration.
5. **Cohen (first name to verify).** Histochemical studies on the regenerating urodele limb. *Dev Biol* 8:48–66 (1963).
   Histochemistry of the regenerating limb. Found only as a citation in a 1966 *J Morphol* paper.
   > ⚠️ Author's first name and species not confirmed.
6. **Johnson EA, Singer M.** A histochemical study of succinic and lactic dehydrogenases in the regenerating forelimb of the adult newt, *Triturus*. *Am Zool* 3(4):511 (1963).
   > ⚠️ Meeting abstract only, not a full paper.
### Eye / lens
 
7. **Yamada T, Karasaki S.** Nuclear RNA synthesis in newt iris cells engaged in regenerative transformation into lens cells. *Dev Biol* 7:595–604 (1963). [*Triturus viridescens*]
   Autoradiography shows that RNA synthesis in the iris cell nucleus rises early in Wolffian lens regeneration, before the cells become lens.
8. **Yamada T, Takata C.** An autoradiographic study of protein synthesis in regenerative tissue transformation of iris into lens in the newt. *Dev Biol* 8:358–369 (Dec 1963). [doi:10.1016/0012-1606(63)90035-6](https://doi.org/10.1016/0012-1606(63)90035-6)
   Companion to entry 7: protein synthesis rises after the RNA increase, supporting a stepwise switch from iris to lens activity.
9. **Eguchi G.** Electron microscopic studies on lens regeneration. I. Mechanism of depigmentation of the iris. *Embryologia* 8(1):45–62 (1963). [newt, probably *Cynops (Triturus) pyrrhogaster*]
   Electron microscopy of how dorsal iris cells lose their pigment before forming a new lens.
   > ⚠️ Start page is cited as 45 or 47 in different sources; species not confirmed.

## Excluded or near-miss items
 
- **Freeman G.** Lens regeneration from the cornea in *Xenopus laevis*. *J Exp Zool* 154:39–65 (1963) — **frog**, not salamander.
- **Hay ED, Revel JP.** Autoradiographic studies of the origin of the basement lamella in *Ambystoma*. *Dev Biol* 7:152–168 (1963) — skin **development**, not clearly regeneration; check if you want it.
- **Thornton CS, Steen TP.** Eccentric blastema formation in aneurogenic limbs. *Dev Biol* 5:328–343 — **1962**.
- **Takata et al. (1964)** on lens antigens; **Singer & Inoue (1964)** on the nerve and epidermal cap — **1964**.
## Caveats
 
- **Coverage is thin.** These are the items I could confirm from publisher records and citing papers. I found **no 1963 tail, spinal cord, heart or brain regeneration papers** in this search, which almost certainly reflects indexing gaps, not a real absence. A 1963 PubMed run and a hand-check of the journal contents above will add more.
- **No abstracts.** Annotations are based on titles and how later papers describe the work. Read the originals before relying on them.
- **Still to verify:** entry 5 (author first name, species), entry 9 (pages, species), and the regeneration content of entry 4.
